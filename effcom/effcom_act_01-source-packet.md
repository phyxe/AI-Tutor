## Source A: Access to Continuity Materials

A learning-continuity task should provide the information required to complete it. A web link may be useful, but it should not be the only route when some learners have limited devices, unstable connections, or shared data access. A short printed source packet can provide equitable access, while an optional link can lead connected learners to the complete document. Teachers should select only the material needed for the learning goal so that the packet remains readable and practical.

## Source B: Limits of Printed Continuity Packets

Printed learning materials can remain usable when internet service is unavailable, but a packet cannot respond to every change after printing. It can also become costly or overwhelming when it reproduces an entire long document. A practical packet should state its date, include only the evidence needed for the task, and tell learners how later corrections or updates will be communicated.

## Source C: Focused Learning Packets

A focused learning packet contains only the source material required for the assigned task. Selecting essential pages reduces printing and helps learners locate evidence, but the packet should still show the source and date so users can identify its origin and recognize when updates may apply.
