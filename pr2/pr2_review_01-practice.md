Use the package's hosted Unit 1 and Unit 2 reference chapters for concepts and worked examples. This file supplies different application tasks. ## Shared Chapter 1 practice case

A fictional Grade 12 class has access to a printed lesson sheet and an optional recorded explanation for four named practice lessons. The class adviser wants a description of which resources learners report using during those four lessons, to inform how future support is prepared. The supplied scenario contains no resource-use counts, grades, test results, or evidence of effectiveness. Participation would require the teacher's later permission and appropriate voluntary, privacy-conscious arrangements. This review requires no real survey, participant names, or access to school records.

Students must retain the descriptive purpose: reported resource use within that class and four-lesson period. They may decide how to express relevant categories/counts, within the scenario. They must not add achievement, causal effectiveness, or an invented relationship as if assigned. This case is separate from the reference chapters' worked examples.

### Supplied instructional evidence and its limits

The practice scenario establishes that two learning-resource forms are available. Availability does not establish actual use or learning benefit. The decision-maker's need is to understand reported use before deciding what support to prepare. These are supplied teaching premises, not verified research findings or an assertion that no prior studies exist.

For the short practice background, identify this source as *PR2 review practice case* (fictional instructional text, version 0.1.0). Do not invent an author, journal, date, DOI, quotation, local statistic, or literature-review claim. A teacher-provided fictional case can ground a rehearsal argument; it cannot substantiate an actual school's prevalence or a real research literature gap. ## Q1. Research and quantitative evidence

Compare “I think learners prefer the recording” with “Ask which of the two available resources each participating learner reports using during the four specified lessons, using the same defined categories.” Explain what makes the second a possible quantitative inquiry, one strength, and one limitation. Distinguish quantitative, qualitative, and mixed-method approaches by the evidence and purpose, not whether a report contains any numbers. Give one practical use of quantitative evidence in a field you understand, without inventing a result.

## Q2. Kinds of quantitative research and claim boundaries

Classify and justify these fictional plans: (a) describe a count of completed practice steps; (b) compare counts across two existing groups without assigning membership; (c) examine whether recorded practice minutes vary with task scores without intervention; (d) assign volunteers randomly to two permitted, equivalent practice conditions and compare an outcome; (e) compare outcomes after a planned intervention in existing classes without random assignment. Explain what additional conditions affect a causal claim and why observation alone does not prove cause. No actual intervention is assigned.

## Q3. Variables, indicators, and data forms

For the shared case, distinguish the population from the variable/focus. Propose a clear indicator, data form, and possible source for reported resource use. Explain categorical versus numerical data using resource type and a count over the four lessons. Explain discrete versus continuous values using a count and elapsed minutes. Show how a variable's role depends on the question; do not force independent/dependent labels on a one-focus descriptive study. Apply nominal, ordinal, interval, and ratio distinctions to suitable examples you explain; numeric codes alone do not establish a quantity. Explain what a confounder could do in the observed relationship case from Q2 rather than claim it was controlled.

## Q4. Research problem and feasibility

Using the shared case, distinguish the broad topic from the specific unknown, explain who could use the information and why, and assess measurability, reachability, manageability, privacy/permission, and claim fit. Explain why “prove the best resource for all Filipino students” exceeds the scenario. Supply a bounded problem statement in your own words, not a copied model.
