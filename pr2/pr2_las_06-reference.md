### Target

Write **conclusions that directly answer the research questions**, formulate practical recommendations proportional to the evidence, and show that the parts of the study agree. The endpoint under the recovery plan is a **Completed Research Evidence Portfolio** with coherent Chapter 5 sections; fully polished Chapters 1–5 typesetting is not the priority unless the school separately requires it.

### Concepts to apply

A **result** reports an observed value or analysis. An **interpretation** explains its meaning. A **conclusion** answers a research question using verified evidence, without adding new data. An **implication** explains why that bounded answer matters. A **recommendation** names an actor and a feasible next action.

Use **Q–E–M–L–C** for conclusions: **Question → Evidence → Meaning → Limits → Conclusion**. Use **A–C–T–I–O–N** for recommendations: **Actor → Concrete action → Tie to evidence → Implementation detail → Observable indicator → Necessary limits**. These are guides for reasoning, not mandatory sentence counts.

### Staged work

1. **Start with the final questions and verified results.** Place each approved research question beside its actual Chapter 4 findings and valid case count. Do not write a conclusion from the title alone or introduce a new result that was never analyzed.
2. **Build a conclusion chain for every question.** Name the evidence, explain the meaningful pattern, identify limits from the design, measure, sample and uncertainty, and state the strongest defensible answer. For correlational research, association is not proof of cause; a nonsignificant result is not proof of equality.
3. **Integrate without exaggerating.** Write a short overall conclusion connecting the per-question answers. Distinguish what the study establishes from what remains unknown; retain mixed, null or unexpected results.
4. **Derive implications and recommendations.** For each useful conclusion, identify who can reasonably benefit, who has authority to act, a modest concrete step, an implementation condition and an observable follow-up indicator. When evidence is weak, recommend limited review or piloting, not sweeping certainty.
5. **Check whole-study coherence.** Trace *title → problem/questions → framework and definitions → literature → design/sample/instrument → actual collection → analysis → findings → conclusion → recommendation*. Resolve contradictions or explain unavoidable limits. Update references, annexes and version records where needed.
6. **Assemble the research evidence portfolio.** Include final versions or clear locations for the approved Chapter 1, theoretical/conceptual framework and Chapter 2, Chapter 3 and actual instrument-quality/permission records, collection/cleaning/analysis evidence, Chapter 4, Chapter 5, and a consequential revision log. Keep participant identities and restricted records in their approved protected locations.

### Major questions

- What is the **direct answer** to each specific research question, based on actual findings?
- Which wording would go beyond the design, instrument, sample or statistical evidence?
- Who can reasonably act on the conclusion, and what concrete action is justified?
- How could the action be monitored without claiming that this study has already proved its effectiveness?
- If someone challenges a conclusion, can we locate the exact table, analysis, data version and original question supporting it?
