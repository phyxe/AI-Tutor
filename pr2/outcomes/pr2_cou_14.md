### Working research title

**Waste Segregation Practices in the School Canteen: Students’ Knowledge, Self-Reported Waste Disposal Practices, and Trash Bin Conditions**

### Research design and focus

The study uses a **descriptive quantitative approach**. It describes three separate aspects of waste segregation in the school canteen of Hawan National High School:

1. Students’ knowledge of proper waste segregation.
2. Students’ self-reported waste disposal practices.
3. The observable condition of trash bins.

The study does not aim to identify causes or test relationships among these three aspects.

### Background of the Study

Proper waste segregation is an important part of solid waste management because it helps reduce the amount of waste that needs to be disposed of and promotes recycling and resource recovery. In the Philippines, Republic Act No. 9003, also known as the Ecological Solid Waste Management Act of 2000, emphasizes waste segregation at the source. It also requires separate containers for different types of waste and proper identification of these containers. The law promotes environmental education to help people understand and practice proper waste management (Republic Act No. 9003, 2001).

Schools also play an important role in teaching students about proper waste segregation. Matsumoto, Saizen, and Shimada (2018) studied elementary schools in Calamba City, Philippines, and examined students’ awareness and participation in waste segregation. Their findings suggested that an environmental education program supported by school eco-centers had a positive impact on students’ awareness and waste segregation practices. This shows that students’ knowledge and participation are relevant aspects to consider when studying waste segregation in schools.

Similarly, Diola (2025) studied solid waste management practices at Mangaldan National High School in Pangasinan. The findings included inconsistent waste segregation, moderate student participation, and problems involving broken or missing trash bins. These findings show why it is useful to examine both students’ reported practices and the observable condition of waste bins in a school setting.

In a school canteen, proper waste segregation is important because students regularly dispose of different types of waste after buying and eating food. However, knowing the correct way to segregate waste does not always mean that students consistently follow it. Likewise, the condition of trash bins does not directly show who disposed of the waste or why different types of waste become mixed. Therefore, students’ knowledge, self-reported waste disposal practices, and trash bin conditions should be examined separately.

This study will focus on describing the current status of waste segregation inside the school canteen of Hawan National High School. It will examine students’ knowledge about proper waste segregation, identify how often they report following proper waste disposal practices, and describe the condition of the trash bins in terms of separate containers, clear labels, and visible waste separation. These three areas will provide information about waste segregation practices and trash bin conditions inside the school canteen without assuming causes or relationships that the study does not examine.

### Statement of the Problem

This study aims to describe the current status of waste segregation practices in the school canteen of Hawan National High School in terms of students’ knowledge, self-reported waste disposal practices, and trash bin conditions.

Specifically, it seeks to answer the following questions:

1. What is the level of students’ knowledge about proper waste segregation in terms of identifying biodegradable and non-biodegradable waste, choosing the appropriate trash bins, and understanding the importance of waste segregation?
2. How often do students report following proper waste disposal practices in the school canteen in terms of using the appropriate trash bins and following waste segregation labels or instructions?
3. What is the condition of the trash bins in the school canteen in terms of the availability of separate bins for different types of waste, clarity of labels, and proper waste segregation?

### Scope and Limitations of the Study

This study will focus only on waste segregation inside the school canteen of Hawan National High School. It will cover students’ knowledge of proper waste segregation, their self-reported waste disposal practices, and the condition of the trash bins. The proposed respondents are selected students from Grades 7 to 11 who regularly buy food at the school canteen and are willing to participate.

The proposed sources of information are a questionnaire and an observation checklist. The population, sample size, and instruments remain subject to the teacher’s review and approval.

The study will not cover other school areas, such as classrooms and the Materials Recovery Facility (MRF). It will not directly observe or identify the actual waste-disposal behavior of each student, identify who caused waste to become mixed, or determine the exact reasons why proper waste segregation may not be followed. It will also not test whether students’ knowledge, self-reported practices, and trash bin conditions are statistically related or whether one causes another.

Because reported practices are based on students’ answers, they may not fully represent their actual behavior. Observations of trash bins can describe only the visible conditions during the observation periods and cannot establish who placed the waste there. The findings will therefore be limited to the selected respondents, the school canteen, and the information gathered through the proposed sources.

### Significance of the Study

This study may be useful to students, canteen staff, and the school administration. For students, the findings may help them better understand the importance of proper waste segregation and reflect on their reported waste disposal practices.

For canteen staff, the findings may provide information about possible improvements in the use, placement, and labeling of trash bins. For the school administration, the results may serve as a guide when considering separate bins, clear labels, and reminders about proper waste segregation.

Overall, the study may provide information that can support efforts to improve cleanliness and waste management in the school canteen. Any action will depend on the decisions of the responsible school personnel.
