### Context

Some students at Hawan National High School belong to more than one school club. When two or more clubs schedule their meetings at the same date and time, a student may face overlapping meeting obligations and may be unable to attend every meeting. This study is based on the researcher's observation of this situation among students with multiple club memberships. The study will examine the frequency of these overlapping schedules and the students' overall club meeting attendance rates during the same reference period.

### Research Problem

The specific concern is whether the frequency of overlapping club meeting schedules is significantly associated with the overall club meeting attendance rates of students who belong to multiple clubs at Hawan National High School. The study will investigate this relationship without claiming that schedule conflicts directly cause absences.

### Proposed Title

**The Relationship Between the Frequency of Overlapping Club Meeting Schedules and the Overall Club Meeting Attendance Rates of Students with Multiple Club Memberships at Hawan National High School**

### Research Questions

**Main Research Question**

Is there a significant relationship between the frequency of overlapping club meeting schedules and the overall club meeting attendance rates of students with multiple club memberships at Hawan National High School during September–October 2026?

**Specific Research Questions**

1. How frequently do students with multiple club memberships experience overlapping club meeting schedules during September–October 2026?
2. What are the overall club meeting attendance rates of students with multiple club memberships during September–October 2026?
3. Is there a significant relationship between the frequency of overlapping club meeting schedules and the overall club meeting attendance rates of these students?

### Variables and Proposed Indicators

| Variable | Role | Operational indicator | Proposed data form/source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency of overlapping club meeting schedules | Independent variable | The number of distinct occasions during September–October 2026 when two or more club meetings that the student belongs to were scheduled for exactly the same date and time and were actually conducted. If three or more clubs meet simultaneously, that is counted as one overlapping occasion. Canceled meetings are not counted as actual overlapping occasions. | Student questionnaire, checked against available club schedules/calendars where possible; record the number of occasions. Any numerical ranges/categories will be set only after reviewing the actual meeting schedules and frequency of meetings. |
| Overall club meeting attendance rate | Dependent variable | (Total meetings attended across the student's clubs ÷ total eligible meetings held across those clubs) × 100. | Club attendance sheets/records supplied by authorized club advisers, supplemented by student self-report only where records are unavailable and the information can be reasonably clarified. Each club component will be labeled according to its source. |

**Attendance calculation rules**

- Include meetings actually held during the reference period when the student was officially a member of the respective club.
- Exclude canceled meetings from the denominator.
- Count simultaneous meetings of different clubs as separate attendance obligations in the attendance-rate denominator, even though one simultaneous time slot counts as one overlapping occasion for the independent variable.
- If no eligible meetings were held for a student during the period, an attendance rate cannot be calculated for that student.
- Do not describe a combined attendance rate as fully record-verified if any part is based on self-reported information.

### Proposed Study Type and Rationale

The study will use a **quantitative, non-experimental correlational design**. It will collect numerical information about overlapping meeting frequency and overall attendance rates, then examine whether the two variables are associated. The researcher will not manipulate club schedules or assign students to conditions. Therefore, the findings may describe the direction and strength of an association but will not establish that schedule conflicts cause absences.

Spearman's rank-order correlation (Spearman's rho) is the proposed statistical method because the frequency measure may be recorded or grouped as ranked/count data and the attendance rate is numerical. The suitability of the final statistical procedure, including the small anticipated number of eligible students, will be confirmed with the research teacher before analysis. A non-significant result will not automatically prove that no relationship exists; the coefficient, p-value, sample size, and limitations will be considered together.

### Target Population and Proposed Access

The target population is the students of Hawan National High School who were officially members of at least two school clubs during September–October 2026. Students with frequent, occasional, or no overlapping meeting schedules will remain eligible so that the study does not exclude students based on the value of the independent variable.

The unit of observation is the individual student. The researcher will request access to official club membership lists from the respective club advisers, subject to school authorization, to identify the eligible population and avoid relying only on self-identification. The eligible population is currently estimated at approximately ten students, but the exact count must be verified from the official lists.

If permitted by the teacher and school, the proposed approach is total population sampling: invite every eligible student to participate. Participation will be voluntary. The actual number who consent and provide usable data will be reported; it will not be assumed in advance. If some eligible students decline or provide incomplete information, this will be disclosed as a possible source of nonresponse bias or a limitation.

### Proposed Data-Gathering Procedure

**Before data collection**

1. Request the required approval from the research teacher and school authorities before contacting participants or accessing records. The proposed plan does not itself grant permission to conduct fieldwork.
2. Coordinate with club advisers to determine whether official membership lists, meeting schedules/calendars, and attendance records for September–October 2026 are available and may be accessed by an authorized person.
3. Use the approved membership lists to identify students who belonged to at least two clubs during the reference period and determine the actual eligible population.
4. Prepare a questionnaire that asks about the respondent's overlapping meeting experiences during the same reference period. Have the research teacher or qualified reviewers examine its clarity, relevance, and alignment with the variables. Any pilot test will be conducted only if the teacher approves a feasible group that will not reduce the eligible study population.
5. Follow school requirements for informed consent and, because participants may be minors, parental/guardian consent and student assent where required. Explain the study purpose, voluntary participation, confidentiality, and the right to decline.

**During data collection**

1. Invite all eligible students if total population sampling is approved. Do not pressure students to participate.
2. Assign respondent codes. Keep the identity-to-code list separate from questionnaires and analysis files, accessible only to authorized persons.
3. Administer the questionnaire to collect each respondent's reported overlapping occasions during September–October 2026. Clarify that one simultaneous time slot involving multiple clubs is one overlap occasion and that canceled meetings are excluded from the overlap count.
4. Request the relevant attendance records from authorized club advisers through the approved school process. Link records to respondent codes only through an authorized, confidential procedure.
5. Calculate each student's overall attendance rate using eligible meetings actually held across the student's club memberships during the reference period.
6. Label each club's attendance information as record-verified or self-reported. If a questionnaire response conflicts with an attendance record, review the dates and available evidence and ask for clarification where appropriate. Do not automatically assume either source is correct. If a discrepancy remains unresolved, mark the affected information as unverified and assess whether the affected case can validly be used.
7. If a student declines, withdraws, or provides incomplete information, respect the decision, do not pressure the student, and document only the non-identifying information necessary to report participation and data completeness.

**After data collection**

1. Check the dataset for missing values, inconsistent dates, duplicate entries, and calculations that cannot be supported.
2. Exclude canceled meetings from the attendance denominator and exclude overlap occasions that do not meet the operational definition. Document unresolved or unusable information transparently.
3. Store coded questionnaires and working data in a secure, access-limited location. Keep any identity-linking record separately and follow the school's retention and disposal requirements.
4. Analyze only the usable paired observations. Present the frequency distribution, attendance-rate distribution, and relationship using appropriate graphs and written explanations, alongside the statistical result when approved by the teacher.
5. Report findings in aggregate form. Do not disclose identifiable student statements or individual attendance details in the research report.

The proposed data-collection period is approximately two weeks, subject to teacher/school approval and the availability of the September–October 2026 records. The reference period for the variables remains September–October 2026; the two-week collection period is not a replacement reference period.
