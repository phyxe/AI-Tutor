## Approved Concept Snapshot

### Context

The researcher heard buyers raise concerns about oily or soft fries and drinks containing too much ice. These reported concerns motivate the inquiry but do not establish actual satisfaction levels.

### Research Problem

Satisfaction with product quality, quantity, and the products overall has not yet been measured among the intended student buyers. The study may help sellers understand buyer preferences and possible areas for improvement.

### Proposed Title

Students’ Level of Satisfaction with the Quality and Quantity of Food and Drinks Sold by Grade 12 TVL Students at Hawan National High School

### Research Questions

Main question: What is the level of satisfaction of students who purchase food or drinks from Grade 12 TVL students at Hawan National High School?

Specific questions:

1. What is the level of students’ satisfaction with the quality of food, in terms of taste, freshness, and texture, and the quality of drinks, in terms of taste and freshness?
2. What is the level of students’ satisfaction with the quantity of food and drinks, in terms of food serving size, amount of drink received, and amount of ice?
3. What is the students’ overall level of satisfaction with the food and drinks sold by Grade 12 TVL students?

### Variables and Proposed Indicators

Main descriptive variable: satisfaction. Quality indicators: food taste/freshness/texture and drink taste/freshness. Quantity indicators: food serving size, drink amount, and ice amount. Include overall satisfaction. Proposed scale: 1 Very dissatisfied, 2 Dissatisfied, 3 Neutral, 4 Satisfied, 5 Very satisfied. Product-specific applicability is open for clarification.

### Proposed Study Type and Rationale

Quantitative descriptive: describe numerical satisfaction ratings, without testing causal effects or requiring an IV/DV pair.

### Target Population and Proposed Access

HNHS students who buy either food or drinks from Grade 12 TVL students at least three days per school week; buying both is not required. Sellers would share a voluntary survey invitation rather than disclose a customer-name list. The reference period and final selection route remain open for clarification.
