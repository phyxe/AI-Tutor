#### Research Title

**Students’ Level of Satisfaction with the Quality and Quantity of Food and Drinks Sold by Grade 12 TVL Students at Hawan National High School**

#### Background of the Study

Food and drinks are among the products that students regularly purchase at school. Their satisfaction may depend on different aspects of the products, such as taste, freshness, texture, serving size, and the amount of drink they receive. Based on the researcher's observations and concerns heard from student buyers, some issues have been noticed regarding certain products sold by Grade 12 TVL students, such as fries that are sometimes too oily or too soft and drinks that may contain too much ice. However, these observations and comments do not establish the actual satisfaction level of student customers.

Students' satisfaction with school food is important to understand because it reflects their experiences and opinions about the products they receive. Santana et al. (2023) reviewed 89 studies on methods used to assess the acceptability of school menus. Their review found that numerical scales were commonly used, with five-point scales being especially prevalent. Questionnaires and interviews were also used to gather students' views. The authors noted that five-point scales may be popular because they are simple, relatively easy to prepare and apply, and understandable to students. This review supports the use of numerical rating scales as one possible way to gather students' opinions about food, although it focuses on school feeding programs rather than products sold by student vendors at Hawan National High School.

Similarly, Lee (2019) examined students' dietary habits, satisfaction with school food service, and attitudes toward school meals. The study surveyed 1,441 students from 58 schools in Gyeonggi-do, South Korea. It found that students with higher satisfaction with food service tended to consume more of their school meals. This finding supports the importance of understanding students' satisfaction with food service. However, the study focused on school meal programs in South Korea. Its findings do not establish the satisfaction level of students who purchase food and drinks from Grade 12 TVL students at Hawan National High School, and the association reported in the study should not be treated as proof that satisfaction alone caused greater meal consumption.

Although previous studies have examined students' satisfaction with school meals and methods for assessing food acceptability, the actual satisfaction level of student customers regarding the quality and quantity of food and drinks sold by Grade 12 TVL students at Hawan National High School has not yet been established in this specific setting. In particular, the opinions of students who regularly purchase these products need to be gathered to understand their actual experiences.

This study focuses on the products sold by Grade 12 TVL students because the researcher wants to understand whether their student customers are satisfied with the products and which aspects may need improvement. The proposed respondents are students who purchase either food or drinks from the Grade 12 TVL students at least three different days during the current school week. They do not need to purchase both types of products, and they will provide feedback only about products they have actually purchased and experienced.

Therefore, this study aims to determine the level of satisfaction of students with the quality and quantity of food and drinks sold by Grade 12 TVL students at Hawan National High School. For food quality, the study will focus on taste, freshness, and texture, while food quantity will focus on serving size. For drinks, quality will focus on taste and freshness, while quantity will focus on the amount of drink received and the amount of ice. Through a survey using a proposed five-point satisfaction rating scale, the study will gather students' ratings and identify which aspects are satisfactory and which may need improvement.

#### Statement of the Problem

This study aims to determine the level of satisfaction of students who purchase food or drinks from Grade 12 TVL students at Hawan National High School.

Specifically, it seeks to answer the following questions:

1. What is the level of students’ satisfaction with the quality of food, in terms of taste, freshness, and texture, and the quality of drinks, in terms of taste and freshness?
2. What is the level of students’ satisfaction with the quantity of food and drinks, in terms of food serving size, amount of drink received, and amount of ice?
3. What is the students’ overall level of satisfaction with the food and drinks sold by Grade 12 TVL students?

For the third question, overall satisfaction will be rated only for the products each respondent has actually purchased and experienced. Students who regularly purchase only food will rate their overall satisfaction with the food; students who regularly purchase only drinks will rate their overall satisfaction with the drinks; and students who purchase both may rate both.

#### Scope and Delimitation

This study will focus on the level of satisfaction of students with the quality and quantity of food and drinks sold by Grade 12 TVL students at Hawan National High School. The proposed respondents will be students who purchase either food or drinks from the Grade 12 TVL students at least three different days during the current school week before answering the survey. They are not required to purchase both food and drinks to qualify as respondents. The current-school-week reference period is the student's proposed choice and must remain subject to the research teacher's feasibility review and the required school permissions before any recruitment.

The study will examine food quality in terms of taste, freshness, and texture, and food quantity in terms of serving size. For drinks, quality will be examined in terms of taste and freshness, while quantity will be considered in terms of the amount of drink received and the amount of ice. Students who purchase only food will answer only the food-related questions, while those who purchase only drinks will answer only the drink-related questions. Students who purchase both may answer questions about both products. Their overall satisfaction ratings will also apply only to the products they have actually purchased and experienced.

The study will use a survey questionnaire with a proposed five-point satisfaction rating scale: 1 – Very Dissatisfied, 2 – Dissatisfied, 3 – Neutral, 4 – Satisfied, and 5 – Very Satisfied. The study will not investigate the actual preparation process, ingredients, or selling prices of the products because its main purpose is to determine students' satisfaction based on their actual experiences as customers.

The study may encounter limitations that could affect the interpretation of the results. Students may have different opinions and preferences, which could lead to varying satisfaction ratings. Some respondents may forget their actual experiences when purchasing the food and drinks, which could affect their answers. Some students may also provide careless or less-than-honest responses. In addition, because the proposed respondents are regular buyers who purchase at least three days during the current school week, the findings may not represent the opinions of students who purchase these products less frequently.
