## Latest available student product — revised proposal draft

**DRAFT / INCOMPLETE.** This is the latest readable proposal available in the accessible files. It is consolidated below and is not a completed implementation report.

### HAWAN READY: A Simple Student Emergency Preparedness Guide
*Individual School-Based Project Proposal*

**Grade & Section:** 12-Courage

### Project Scope
This is an individual project led by the student proponent. It will involve only a small, teacher-approved group of approximately 10–15 high school students, rather than the entire school. The project will not conduct school-wide drills, facility inspections, or emergency-response operations.

### Specific School Issue
Some high school students may be unsure about basic actions to take during common emergencies such as earthquakes, fires, typhoons, and floods, especially before, during, and after an emergency.

### Rationale
Knowing simple and appropriate safety actions can help students respond more calmly and follow school instructions. This project will focus on sharing easy-to-understand reminders with a small group, using information checked against existing school DRRM materials or by a teacher.

### General Objective
To improve the basic emergency-preparedness knowledge of a small group of high school students through a simple guide and short awareness activity.

### Specific Objectives
1. Find out what participants already know through a short pre-assessment.
2. Identify which basic emergency topics need more explanation.
3. Prepare a simple, student-friendly guide using free digital tools or available paper.
4. Conduct one short, teacher-approved discussion or activity with the selected participants.
5. Compare pre- and post-assessment answers to see whether understanding improved.

### Proposed Solution
The student proponent will create a short HAWAN READY guide containing basic reminders for earthquakes, fires, typhoons, and floods. The guide may be shared digitally or printed only when necessary. The proponent will then facilitate one short awareness activity for a small approved group, using questions or simple scenario cards. All safety instructions will be checked with a teacher or existing school DRRM materials before use.

### Target Participants and Beneficiaries
Approximately 10–15 high school students, subject to teacher approval and availability. The project is designed for a small group that one student can reasonably prepare for and facilitate.

### Activity Plan

| Activity | Person responsible | Resources | Schedule | Expected output |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Prepare a short pre-assessment and ask the teacher to approve the plan | Student proponent | Phone/computer; paper or digital form | Week 1 | Approved questions and baseline responses |
| Review answers and verify safety information | Student proponent, with brief teacher guidance | Assessment results; existing school DRRM materials | Week 1 | Priority topics and checked information |
| Create a short digital guide | Student proponent | Free digital tool; existing device | Week 1–2 | Completed digital guide |
| Conduct one short awareness activity for 10–15 approved students | Student proponent, with teacher approval/supervision | Guide; simple scenario questions | Week 2 | Small-group activity completed |
| Give a post-assessment and summarize results | Student proponent | Short questionnaire or digital form | Week 2–3 | Pre/post comparison and reflection |

### Budget and Resources

| Item | Estimated cost | Cost-reduction plan |
|---|---:|---|
| Phone/computer and free digital tool | ₱0 | Use an available device and free software |
| Digital sharing of guide | ₱0 | Share a file instead of printing |
| Paper/pen for a few questionnaires, if needed | ₱0–₱30 | Use available paper or a free digital form |
| **Total estimated cash cost** | **₱0–₱30** | No purchases required if materials are already available |

### Monitoring and Evaluation
A short pre-assessment will be given before the activity and a comparable post-assessment afterward. The proponent will compare the number of correct answers and note which topics remain unclear. No student names or sensitive personal information are needed; anonymous codes may be used.

### Risks and Safeguards
- Incorrect or outdated advice: check the guide with a teacher or official school DRRM materials.
- Limited attendance: coordinate the schedule with the teacher and use the actual number of participants.
- Printing costs: prioritize a digital copy and print only a few essential pages if needed.
- Safety: do not conduct an independent emergency drill or ask participants to perform risky actions.
- Privacy: collect only assessment answers needed for project evaluation.

### Sustainability
The digital guide can be kept by the proponent and shared with the participating students, subject to teacher approval. If the activity is useful, the teacher may decide whether it can be reused. Any expansion beyond the small group is not part of this individual project and would require separate approval.

### Expected Outputs
1. One simple HAWAN READY digital guide.
2. One short awareness activity for a small approved group.
3. Pre- and post-assessment summaries.
4. A brief reflection on what worked and what should be improved.

### Feasibility
The project can be completed in about two to three weeks, depending on the school schedule. It uses simple materials, requires no special equipment, and does not require the proponent to manage the whole school.

### Project Title Note
The original HAWAN READY theme is retained, but the project has been narrowed to a small, individual activity. It is not a proposal to implement a program across the entire school.
