> # CHAPTER 1 — THE PROBLEM AND ITS BACKGROUND
>
> ## Title
>
> **Relationship Between Seating Position and Classroom Attention Among Grade 11 – Knowledge Students**
>
> ## 1.1 Background of the Study
>
> Classroom attention is important because it helps students listen to the teacher's explanation and follow the lesson. When students pay attention, they can understand the information being discussed and learn new things. It also helps them stay focused during class discussions.
>
> Students sit in different areas of the classroom, such as the front, middle, and back. Their seating positions are worth examining because their surroundings may be different and may be related to how they listen and focus during the teacher's discussion. However, students may have different levels of attention regardless of where they sit.
>
> Published studies provide information about the possible relationship between seating position and student attention. Shernoff et al. (2017) conducted a study involving 407 university students in large lecture classes. Their findings showed that students reported lower levels of attention, engagement, and classroom experience when sitting at the back compared with those sitting in the middle or front. However, the study measured students' self-reported experiences rather than attention through direct classroom observation.
>
> In contrast, Seet et al. (2022) examined seating arrangements and class engagement among undergraduate medical students in Singapore. Their findings showed that seating distance did not significantly affect engagement among experienced students. However, novice students showed decreased engagement when they moved farther from the tutor. The study also involved a specially designed team-based learning classroom, which had features different from an ordinary classroom.
>
> The two studies had different findings because they involved different students and classroom conditions. This shows that seating position may not have the same relationship with attention in every classroom. Their findings may not automatically apply to all students because each classroom has its own learning environment and conditions.
>
> This study will focus on the relationship between seating position and classroom attention among Grade 11 – Knowledge students because it aims to find out whether their seating positions are related to how they listen and focus during the teacher's discussion. Specifically, it will examine whether students sitting in the front, middle, and back have different levels of classroom attention.
>
> ## 1.2 Statement of the Problem
>
> This study aims to determine whether there is a relationship between students' seating positions and their classroom attention during teacher-led discussions.
>
> **Main Research Question**
>
> Is there a relationship between students' seating positions and their classroom attention during teacher-led discussions?
>
> **Specific Research Questions**
>
> 1. What is the level of classroom attention among students sitting at the front, middle, and back of the classroom?
> 2. What is the classroom attention of students during the teacher's explanation in terms of listening, focus, and avoiding distractions?
>
> ## 1.3 Scope and Limitations of the Study
>
> This study will focus on the 17 students of Grade 11 – Knowledge at Hawan National High School during School Year 2026–2027. It will examine their seating positions and classroom attention during the teacher's discussions.
>
> The seating positions will be classified as front, middle, and back. Classroom attention will be observed through three indicators: listening to the teacher, focusing on the lesson, and avoiding distractions. These behaviors will be rated using an observation checklist and the following scale:
>
> - **1 – Rarely**
> - **2 – Sometimes**
> - **3 – Always**
>
> The planned observations will cover three meetings. Each student will be assigned a number, and their seating position and attention ratings will be recorded separately for each meeting. If a student changes seats, the actual seating position during that meeting will be recorded.
>
> This study will not include students' grades, test scores, or overall academic performance. It will only focus on their seating positions and classroom attention during the teacher's discussion. Students' behavior outside the discussion will also not be observed.
>
> The observations may be affected if some students are absent or if their behavior cannot be clearly seen during the discussion. Students may also change their seating positions in each meeting. If a student is absent, the record will indicate "Absent" and the ratings will be left blank. If a behavior cannot be clearly observed, the affected indicator will be marked "Not Observed" and its rating will be left blank instead of assigning a guessed rating.
>
> The findings may not represent all Grade 11 students or students from other schools because the study will only involve 17 students from one class. The results will describe the relationship between seating position and classroom attention among the selected students.
>
> Since this study uses a quantitative correlational design, it cannot establish that seating position causes students to become more or less attentive. Other factors may also be related to students' classroom attention.
>
> ## 1.4 Significance of the Study
>
> **Students.** This study may help Grade 11 – Knowledge students become more aware of their seating positions and classroom attention. They may learn whether their seating positions are related to how they listen and focus during the teacher's discussion. This may help them think about ways to improve their attention in class.
>
> **Teacher.** This study may help the teacher understand how students' seating positions are related to their classroom attention. The findings may help the teacher become more aware of students' listening and focus in different seating areas.
>
> **Future Researchers.** This study may help future researchers who want to examine seating positions and classroom attention. They may use this study as a reference for their own research or explore other factors that may be related to students' attention.
>
