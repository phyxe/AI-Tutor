#### Context

The proposed study will be conducted at Hawan Grande Covered Court. The researcher’s initial observation was that waste bins may be limited in some areas and that some waste may be improperly disposed of after basketball activities. These are personal observations that motivate the study; they are not verified findings or measurements of the actual number or sufficiency of waste bins.

The study will focus on male Junior High School basketball players who use the covered court. It will examine how often they report encountering available waste bins and how often they report practicing proper and improper waste disposal.

#### Research Problem

The study aims to determine whether the reported availability of waste bins is significantly related to the waste disposal practices of male Junior High School basketball players at Hawan Grande Covered Court.

The concern is that waste disposal practices may differ even when students encounter available bins. The study will examine reported proper and improper practices without assuming that bin availability causes a particular behavior. #### Proposed Title

**The Relationship Between the Observed Availability of Waste Bins and Waste Disposal Practices Among Male Junior High School Basketball Players at Hawan Grande Covered Court**

#### Research Questions

**Main Research Question**

Is there a significant relationship between the observed availability of waste bins and the waste disposal practices of male Junior High School basketball players at Hawan Grande Covered Court?

**Specific Research Questions**

1. What is the reported frequency of encountering available waste bins among the respondents?
2. What is the reported frequency of their proper and improper waste disposal practices?
3. Is there a significant relationship between the observed availability of waste bins and (a) proper waste disposal practices and (b) improper waste disposal practices?

#### Variables and Proposed Indicators

##### Variable 1: Observed Availability of Waste Bins

This variable will be measured through respondents’ self-reported frequency of encountering waste bins that are present and usable during their usual basketball activities at Hawan Grande Covered Court. It will not measure the actual number or sufficiency of waste bins.

Proposed questionnaire statements:

1. I see waste bins at Hawan Grande Covered Court when I play basketball.
2. I can find waste bins near the areas where basketball players usually stay.
3. I find waste bins available when I need to throw away my waste.
4. I can easily reach the waste bins during my basketball activities.
5. Waste bins remain available throughout my basketball activities.

##### Variable 2: Waste Disposal Practices

This variable will be described using separate groups of proper and improper disposal indicators. The respondent will answer based on their usual experiences whenever they play basketball at Hawan Grande Covered Court.

**Proposed proper-disposal statements**

1. I throw my waste into the designated waste bins.
2. I keep my waste with me when no waste bin is available.
3. I make sure my waste is properly disposed of after playing basketball.

**Proposed improper-disposal statements**

4. I leave my waste on the ground after eating or drinking.
5. I leave my waste where I am even when a waste bin is available nearby.

**Proposed response scale for all statements**

- 5 – Always
- 4 – Often
- 3 – Sometimes
- 2 – Rarely
- 1 – Never

**Proposed interpretation**

For the availability items, a higher score indicates more frequent reported encounters with available waste bins. For proper-disposal items, a higher score indicates more frequent proper practices. For improper-disposal items, a higher score indicates more frequent improper practices. Proper and improper disposal scores will be examined separately; their raw scores will not be combined into one total. The proposed analysis will examine the relationship between availability and proper disposal, and between availability and improper disposal, separately.

The questionnaire statements, scoring, and statistical treatment remain subject to the research teacher’s review and approval before use. The five availability statements also require teacher review for clarity and possible overlap.

#### Proposed Study Type and Rationale

The study will use a **quantitative, non-experimental descriptive-correlational design**. It is quantitative because it will gather numerical responses through a structured questionnaire and analyze those responses. It is correlational because the purpose is to determine whether the two variables are significantly related. The researcher will not manipulate or control the availability of waste bins or the respondents’ disposal practices.

The study can identify associations only. It cannot establish that bin availability causes proper or improper disposal, or that disposal behavior causes a shortage of bins.

### CHAPTER 1 — THE PROBLEM AND ITS BACKGROUND

#### Research Title

**The Relationship Between the Observed Availability of Waste Bins and Waste Disposal Practices Among Male Junior High School Basketball Players at Hawan Grande Covered Court**

#### Background of the Study

Proper waste disposal is important in maintaining cleanliness in public places. At Hawan Grande Covered Court, the researcher's initial observations indicate that some waste may be improperly disposed of after basketball activities. The researcher has also observed that players may dispose of their waste properly when waste bins are nearby, while some waste may be left behind or discarded elsewhere when bins are difficult to reach. These are the researcher's initial observations and have not yet been established as measured findings about all court users.

Previous studies have highlighted the importance of waste receptacle availability and placement. Bator, Bryan, and Schultz (2011) interviewed waste disposers in 14 outdoor settings across eight U.S. states. Their abstract reports that participants were less likely to litter in locations where more receptacles were available and where receptacles could be easily reached. This finding supports examining reported availability and accessibility in relation to disposal practices. However, it does not establish what happens among the basketball players at Hawan Grande Covered Court.

Similarly, Leeabai et al. (2019) examined how trash-bin setting conditions relate to waste collection and separation. Their abstract reports that increasing the distance of bins outside the walking path significantly decreased the collection of combustible waste, while distance along the walking path did not have a significant effect on waste collection. This suggests that the placement of bins in relation to people's usual paths may matter. However, the study was conducted in a different setting and does not prove that the same relationship exists among the target respondents in this study.

Although these studies provide evidence about receptacle availability and placement, they do not establish whether the reported availability of waste bins is significantly related to the waste disposal practices of male Junior High School basketball players at Hawan Grande Covered Court. This is the specific relationship that the present study seeks to examine. The study will consider proper and improper disposal practices separately and will not assume that bin availability causes a particular behavior.

The findings may provide barangay officials with information that can support discussions or decisions about waste management at the covered court. They may also encourage basketball players to practice proper waste disposal and serve as positive examples to other young people who use the area. Other residents and visitors who use the multipurpose covered court may also benefit from a cleaner environment. The study specifically aims to determine whether a significant relationship exists between the observed availability of waste bins and the waste disposal practices of the target respondents.

#### Statement of the Problem

This study aims to determine whether the observed availability of waste bins is significantly related to the waste disposal practices of male Junior High School basketball players at Hawan Grande Covered Court. It will examine respondents' reported frequency of encountering available waste bins and their reported frequency of proper and improper waste disposal practices. The study will use a quantitative, non-experimental descriptive-correlational approach; therefore, its findings will identify relationships and will not establish cause and effect.

Specifically, this study seeks to answer the following questions:

1. What is the reported frequency of encountering available waste bins among the respondents?
2. What is the reported frequency of their proper and improper waste disposal practices?
3. Is there a significant relationship between the observed availability of waste bins and (a) proper waste disposal practices and (b) improper waste disposal practices?

#### Scope and Delimitation

This study will focus on the relationship between the observed availability of waste bins and the waste disposal practices of male Junior High School basketball players at Hawan Grande Covered Court. The availability variable will be based on respondents' self-reported frequency of encountering waste bins that are present and usable during their usual basketball activities. It will not measure the actual number or objective sufficiency of waste bins. Waste disposal practices will include proper and improper practices, which will be considered separately.

The proposed respondents are male Junior High School students of Hawan National High School who are 13–19 years old, reside in Hawan Grande, play basketball at Hawan Grande Covered Court approximately three to five times a week, and have experience disposing of waste in the area. The researcher proposes to invite all students who meet these criteria, subject to confirming the eligible population and obtaining the required permissions and consent. Students who do not meet the stated criteria will be excluded.

The study will be conducted within the two-week period indicated by the research adviser; the exact dates have not yet been specified in the available record. The study will rely on respondents' reported experiences and answers. Because it is correlational, it will not determine that the availability of waste bins causes proper or improper disposal, nor will it establish that disposal behavior causes a shortage of bins.

Possible limitations include class suspensions, school activities, respondent absences, nonresponse, and the unwillingness or unavailability of some eligible students to participate. These circumstances may affect the number of completed responses within the planned period and are not fully under the researcher's control. Required school permission, parent or guardian consent for minors, and student assent must be secured before data collection.
