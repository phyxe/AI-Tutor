> # CHAPTER 1 — THE PROBLEM AND ITS BACKGROUND
>

>
> ## Research Title
>
> The Relationship Between the Perceived Accessibility of Waste Bins and Waste Disposal Practices Among Male Junior High School Basketball Players at Hawan Grande Covered Court
>
> ## Background of the Study
>
> Proper waste disposal is important in maintaining cleanliness in public places. At Hawan Grande Covered Court, the researcher's initial observations indicate that some waste may be improperly disposed of after basketball activities. The researcher has also observed that players may dispose of their waste properly when waste bins are easy to reach, while some waste may be left behind or discarded elsewhere when bins are difficult to access. These are the researcher's initial observations and have not yet been established as measured findings about all court users.
>
> Previous studies have highlighted the importance of waste receptacle availability and placement. Bator, Bryan, and Schultz (2011) interviewed waste disposers in 14 outdoor settings across eight U.S. states. Their abstract reports that participants were less likely to litter in locations where more receptacles were available and where receptacles could be easily reached. This finding supports examining the accessibility of waste bins in relation to disposal practices. However, it does not establish what happens among the basketball players at Hawan Grande Covered Court.
>
> Similarly, Leeabai et al. (2019) examined how trash-bin setting conditions relate to waste collection and separation. Their abstract reports that increasing the distance of bins outside the walking path significantly decreased the collection of combustible waste, while distance along the walking path did not have a significant effect on waste collection. This suggests that the placement of bins in relation to people's usual paths may matter. However, the study was conducted in a different setting and does not prove that the same relationship exists among the target respondents in this study.
>
> Although these studies provide evidence about receptacle availability and placement, they do not establish whether the perceived accessibility of waste bins is significantly related to the waste disposal practices of male Junior High School basketball players at Hawan Grande Covered Court. In this study, accessibility will focus on the perceived sufficiency of the number and proper placement of waste bins, particularly how easily players can reach and use them during their usual basketball activities. The study will consider proper and improper disposal practices separately and will not assume that accessibility causes a particular behavior.
>
> The findings may provide barangay officials with information that can support discussions or decisions about waste-bin provision and placement at the covered court. They may also encourage basketball players to practice proper waste disposal and serve as positive examples to other young people who use the area. Other residents and visitors who use the multipurpose covered court may also benefit from a cleaner environment. The study specifically aims to determine whether a significant relationship exists between the perceived accessibility of waste bins and the waste disposal practices of the target respondents.
>
> ## Statement of the Problem
>
> This study aims to determine whether the perceived accessibility of waste bins is significantly related to the waste disposal practices of male Junior High School basketball players at Hawan Grande Covered Court. It will examine the respondents' perceptions of the number and placement of waste bins, as well as their reported frequency of proper and improper waste disposal practices. The study will use a quantitative, non-experimental descriptive-correlational approach; therefore, its findings will identify relationships and will not establish cause and effect.
>
> Specifically, this study seeks to answer the following questions:
>
> 1. What is the level of perceived accessibility of waste bins in terms of: (a) number of waste bins; and (b) proper placement of waste bins?
> 2. How frequently do the respondents practice proper waste disposal?
> 3. How frequently do the respondents practice improper waste disposal?
> 4. Is there a significant relationship between the perceived accessibility of waste bins and: (a) proper waste disposal practices; and (b) improper waste disposal practices?
>
> ## Scope and Delimitation
>
> This study will focus on the relationship between the perceived accessibility of waste bins and the waste disposal practices of male Junior High School basketball players at Hawan Grande Covered Court. The perceived accessibility of waste bins will be examined in terms of the perceived sufficiency of their number and their proper placement, particularly how easily respondents can reach and use them during their usual basketball activities. This variable will be measured through the respondents' questionnaire answers. Waste disposal practices will include proper and improper practices, which will be considered separately.
>
> The proposed respondents are male Junior High School students of Hawan National High School who are 13–19 years old, reside in Hawan Grande, and play basketball at Hawan Grande Covered Court approximately three to five times a week. The frequency criterion is based on the researcher's initial observations and will be verified during participant screening. Respondents must also have experience using the covered court and disposing of waste in the area. The researcher proposes to invite all students who meet these criteria, subject to confirming the eligible population and obtaining the required permissions and consent. Students who do not meet the stated criteria will be excluded.
>
> The study will be conducted within the two-week period indicated by the research adviser; the exact dates have not yet been specified. The study will rely on respondents' reported perceptions and experiences through their questionnaire answers. Because it is correlational, it will not determine that the accessibility of waste bins causes proper or improper disposal, nor will it establish that disposal behavior causes problems with waste-bin accessibility.
>
> Possible limitations include class suspensions, school activities, respondent absences, nonresponse, and the unwillingness or unavailability of some eligible students to participate. These circumstances may affect the number of completed responses within the planned period and are not fully under the researcher's control. Required school permission, parent or guardian consent for minors, and student assent must be secured before data collection.
