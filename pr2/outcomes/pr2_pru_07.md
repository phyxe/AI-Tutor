PR2 ACTIVITY 1 — CONCEPT NOTE
Working title: Procrastination and Late Module Submissions Among Selected Grade 11 Students of the Wisdom Section at Hawan National High School

### Context

Based on my general observations, some students become distracted by cellphone use, have difficulty understanding their modules, or have personal responsibilities that may delay their schoolwork. This motivated me to investigate whether procrastination is significantly related to late module submissions among selected Grade 11 students. These are observations and possible contributing factors, not established findings about all students.

### Research Problem

This study aims to determine whether procrastination is significantly related to late module submissions among selected Grade 11 students from the Wisdom section of Hawan National High School. It also seeks to describe the common reasons students report for procrastinating when completing their modules.

### Proposed Title

Procrastination and Late Module Submissions Among Selected Grade 11 Students of the Wisdom Section at Hawan National High School

### Research Questions

1. What is the level of procrastination among selected Grade 11 students from the Wisdom section?
2. What are the common reasons why these students procrastinate when completing their modules?
3. What is the extent of late module submissions among the selected students?
4. Is there a significant relationship between procrastination and late module submissions among the selected students?

### Variables and Proposed Indicators

**Procrastination.** This will be measured using a printed questionnaire about behaviors such as delaying the start of modules, waiting until deadlines are near, becoming distracted while doing schoolwork, and having difficulty following a study schedule. Proposed response choices are Always, Often, Sometimes, and Never, with numerical values assigned for analysis. The questionnaire items and scoring must be reviewed and validated by qualified validators and approved by the research teacher before use.

**Common reasons for procrastination.** The questionnaire will also ask about possible reasons students report, such as cellphone or social-media distractions, difficulty understanding modules, personal responsibilities, lack of motivation, and time-management difficulties. These responses will be used to describe reported reasons; they will not establish that any one factor caused late submissions.

**Late module submissions.** If access is available and authorized, school submission records will be used to determine each participant's number of late modules and total assigned modules during the most recent completed grading period. The late-submission percentage may be calculated as:

Late-submission percentage = (number of late modules / total assigned modules) × 100

This calculation will only be used when both counts are available and the records use a consistent definition of the deadline and submission period.

### Proposed Study Type and Rationale

The study will use a quantitative correlational research design. It will measure students' reported procrastination and their late module submissions, then examine whether the two variables are statistically related. The study will observe existing behaviors and records rather than manipulate students' behavior. A relationship, if found, will not prove that procrastination causes late submissions. Other circumstances may also be relevant.

### Target Population and Proposed Access

The intended participants are Grade 11 students from the Wisdom section of Hawan National High School who voluntarily agree to participate and meet the study's eligibility requirements. The unit of observation is each participating student. The proposed approach is voluntary participation among eligible students in that section. Because participation is limited to willing students from one section, the findings cannot automatically be generalized to all Grade 11 students or other schools.

Permission from the research teacher and school principal will be requested before recruitment or access to school records. Any required parent or guardian consent and student assent will be obtained in accordance with school requirements and applicable rules. No recruitment, questionnaire administration, or record access will occur before the required approvals are granted.

### Proposed Data-Gathering Procedure

**Before data gathering:** The researcher will seek the required school approvals, ask qualified validators to review the questionnaire, revise unclear items, and follow the teacher's instructions for validation and consent. The exact record period and availability of assigned-module and submission-date records will be confirmed with authorized school personnel.

**During data gathering:** Eligible students will be informed about the study's purpose, procedures, voluntary nature, and their right to decline or withdraw without penalty. Printed questionnaires will be given only after the required consent and assent procedures are completed. Participants will be assigned codes rather than having their names displayed in the research dataset. If authorized records are available, the researcher will use the same participant codes to match questionnaire data with the relevant submission information. The code key, if one is needed, will be kept separately and securely, with access restricted to authorized persons.

**After data gathering:** Responses and authorized record information will be checked for completeness and accuracy. Missing answers will not be invented. Incomplete data will be handled according to the research teacher's guidance. The data will be organized and analyzed using the statistical procedures approved by the research teacher. Names and identifiable details will not appear in the report. Research information will be stored securely, accessed only by authorized persons, used only for the approved academic purpose, and retained or disposed of according to school requirements and applicable data-protection rules.

### Scope and Limitations

The study is limited to participating Grade 11 students from the Wisdom section of Hawan National High School. Voluntary participation may result in a group that does not represent every student in the section. The study examines the relationship between procrastination and late module submissions and describes reasons reported by participants; it does not establish cause and effect. The use of school records depends on their availability, consistency, and authorized access. The questionnaire must be validated before it is administered.
