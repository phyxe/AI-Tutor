### Context

Mobile games are commonly played by students, including during school hours. When a student plays a mobile game while the teacher is discussing a lesson, the game may compete with the student's attention to the discussion. This study will focus on Grade 10 students at Hawan National High School and will examine the relationship between how often they play mobile games while the teacher is discussing and their attention during class discussions.

The study will include students who play mobile games and students who do not. This will help describe the pattern among the selected students without assuming that every student is a mobile gamer.

### Research Problem

The study will determine whether there is a significant relationship between the frequency of playing mobile games while the teacher is discussing and the level of students' attention during class discussions among selected Grade 10 students at Hawan National High School.

The study will examine an association between the two variables. It will not claim that playing mobile games directly causes a change in attention.

### Proposed Title

**The Relationship Between the Frequency of Playing Mobile Games While the Teacher Is Discussing and the Attention of Grade 10 Students at Hawan National High School**

### Research Questions

1. How often do Grade 10 students at Hawan National High School play mobile games while the teacher is discussing during class discussions?
2. What is the level of attention of Grade 10 students at Hawan National High School during class discussions?
3. Is there a significant relationship between the frequency of playing mobile games while the teacher is discussing and the level of students' attention?

### Variables and Proposed Indicators

**Independent variable: Frequency of playing mobile games while the teacher is discussing**

The proposed reference period is the past two weeks. The questionnaire will ask each respondent:

- How many teacher-led class discussions did you attend during the past two weeks?
- In how many of those discussions did you play mobile games while your teacher was discussing?

The proposed gaming-frequency percentage will be calculated as:

Gaming Frequency (%) = (Number of discussions in which the student played mobile games ÷ Number of teacher-led discussions attended) × 100

A student who attended at least one discussion but did not play mobile games during any of them will have a result of 0%. Students who attended no teacher-led class discussions during the reference period will not be included in the analysis because the percentage cannot be calculated when the number of discussions attended is zero.

**Dependent variable: Level of attention during class discussions**

The proposed attention indicators are:

1. Listening to the teacher
2. Focusing on the lesson
3. Following the classroom discussion
4. Participating in class discussions

The questionnaire will use a five-point agreement scale:

| Score | Response |
|---:|---|
| 5 | Strongly Agree |
| 4 | Agree |
| 3 | Neutral |
| 2 | Disagree |
| 1 | Strongly Disagree |

The attention items will refer to the respondent's experience during teacher-led class discussions within the same past-two-weeks reference period. The final questionnaire wording and the method for combining item responses into an attention score will be reviewed by the research teacher before data collection.

### Proposed Study Type and Rationale

This study will use a quantitative, correlational approach. It is quantitative because information will be collected through a structured questionnaire and summarized numerically. It is correlational because the study will examine whether gaming frequency and students' attention are related. It will not test or claim a cause-and-effect relationship.

Proposed descriptive statistics include frequency and percentage for relevant responses and mean and standard deviation for attention ratings, subject to teacher review. Spearman's rank correlation is a proposed method for examining the relationship between gaming frequency and attention ratings because the attention responses use an ordered agreement scale. The final statistical treatment will be confirmed with the research teacher after the questionnaire and data plan are reviewed.

### Target Population and Proposed Access

The target population is Grade 10 students at Hawan National High School. The proposed respondents will be 20 students from two selected Grade 10 sections:

- Section 1: 10 students
- Section 2: 10 students

The two sections will be selected purposively. Within each selected section, 10 eligible students will be selected randomly. The total number of Grade 10 students enrolled in the two sections has not yet been verified and will not be estimated or invented.

To be eligible, a student must:

- Be officially enrolled in Grade 10 at Hawan National High School.
- Belong to one of the two selected sections.
- Have attended at least one teacher-led class discussion during the past two weeks.
- Be willing to participate voluntarily.

Students may be mobile gamers or non-gamers. A student who does not play mobile games may still participate and may report zero gaming discussions. Students who attended no teacher-led class discussions during the reference period will not be included because the frequency percentage cannot be calculated for them.

If a selected student refuses to participate, the refusal will be respected and another eligible student will be randomly selected from the same section. Access to students and the conduct of the study will depend on school approval, teacher approval, and the required consent and assent procedures. These permissions have not yet been obtained.

### Proposed Data-Gathering Procedure

**Before data collection**

1. Request the required approval from the school administration and the research teacher.
2. Prepare the printed questionnaire and submit it for research-teacher review. Revise it if needed and conduct a pilot test if the teacher requires one.
3. Identify eligible students from the two selected Grade 10 sections and randomly select 10 students from each section.
4. Explain the study's purpose and what participation involves. Obtain the required consent and assent, including parent or guardian consent where required by the school.
5. Make clear that participation is voluntary and that students may refuse without penalty.

**During data collection**

1. Distribute the printed questionnaires during vacant time, with the teacher's approval and when permitted.
2. Allow respondents approximately 10–15 minutes to answer.
3. Collect each questionnaire immediately after the respondent finishes.
4. Do not ask respondents to write their names. Keep responses anonymous.

**After data collection**

1. Check the questionnaires for unanswered required items.
2. Exclude a questionnaire from the statistical analysis if at least one required item is unanswered. Do not guess or fill in missing answers.
3. Do not include students who attended zero teacher-led class discussions during the past two weeks in the analysis of gaming-frequency percentage.
4. Organize and encode the usable responses, then summarize only group results.
5. Keep the collected questionnaires in a sealed envelope and store them in a locked drawer or box accessible only to the researcher.
6. Use the responses only for this research. Do not share, show, or discuss individual answers with other students or teachers.
7. Report overall patterns and group results only, not individual answers.
8. After the data have been encoded and the study is complete, shred the paper questionnaires to protect respondents' privacy.

The actual data-gathering schedule, final questionnaire, permissions, consent/assent process, and final statistical treatment remain subject to review and approval by the school and research teacher. No fieldwork or respondent contact is authorized by this concept note.
