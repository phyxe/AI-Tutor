### Population rule: criteria first, complete enumeration below 100

This is Sir Francis's **Activity 3 teaching rule**, not a universal statistical claim that 100 determines the correct method. It overrides generic sampling prompts in the LAS/module references; those resources do not reopen a below-100 sampling lesson or create an authority conflict. Use the total membership of the actual defined study population, not a desired response count, accessible fragment, pilot count or unsupported estimate. First reuse the recorded size/basis or ask it within the open population target. A member must be identifiable through relevant criteria: who/what qualifies, inclusion/exclusion conditions, setting/period or status where needed, and an actual roster/record/eligibility-check route. Ask for the rule and aggregate count, not names or an identifiable roster.

- **Known population of 1–99 eligible members:** use a complete-enumeration/whole-population invitation plan. Do not offer random/convenience/stratified sampling, ask students to choose/justify/compare sampling methods, calculate a sample size, introduce formulas/margins of error, or ask why they are not sampling. Sampling-only ledger fields are Not applicable — complete enumeration under this activity's rule; no justification interview. In the clean chapter use **Population and Participant Coverage** instead of forcing a Sampling Procedure heading or a fixed “sample” number. The student must understand membership criteria, how every eligible member will be identified/invited under actual authority, and how refusal, inaccessibility or incomplete responses will be recorded. Invitation of all does not guarantee response by all; never turn the population count into an achieved count or claim stronger validity/generalization.
- **Population size unknown, contradictory or zero:** clarify criteria/count basis within budget, record the unresolved issue and do not invent a count or launch a sampling lesson. Keep available independent work and submit honestly; an empty group needs a feasible population decision, not a sample formula.
- **Known population of 100 or more:** there is no automatic instruction to sample. Use actual teacher constraints/design/access; discuss a justified sampling route only if relevant, or retain a whole-population plan when appropriate. The below-100 exclusion does not silently extend to exactly 100.

If an older teacher-locked plan conflicts, retain the historical baseline and record the proposed population-coverage correction for the teacher's after-submission decision; do not conduct a sampling interview or invent clearance. This population rule does not cancel instrument validation/reliability requirements. Any authorized pretest/pilot is a separate quality-check activity, not permission to teach main-study sampling below 100 or assume participants may be contacted.

### Validation and reliability: understand and prepare the next process

The intended tool endpoint is a **draft prepared for validation and applicable reliability testing**, not a tool the AI certifies as validated, reliable or cleared for main collection. Students must understand why they need the checks and how to use their draft next. Completed external validation/pilot/reliability results are **not required just to submit this draft or demonstrate process understanding**; their true status remains Not yet conducted/Pending or Not applicable with a specific reason.

Use the actual tool to establish this sequence, retaining prior evidence and staying within A3-Q4's allowance:

1. Explain intended construct/indicator/response use: validity concerns evidence for that interpretation and coverage, not a signature or attractive wording. Prepare the draft/version, question–indicator blueprint and a review route with appropriate expertise/role; identify what will be checked for relevance, clarity, response/scoring fit and fairness/privacy.
2. Explain a respondent-understanding/pretest check versus a whole-process pilot: the former checks how an item/direction is interpreted; the latter checks the authorized administration, timing, response, scoring/export and handling process. A pilot is not the main study or evidence of its findings. Any people/records require the actual applicable authorization and safeguards.
3. Explain reliability as suitable consistency/precision evidence for the actual scores/records and intended use. Select a justified approach with teacher/adviser guidance where needed; do not prescribe Cronbach's alpha for every questionnaire, a coefficient for a lone mixed item, a universal cutoff or fixed pilot number. Knowing a coefficient's name is not process understanding; statistical calculation on invented data is prohibited.
4. Explain what to record and do afterward: actual reviewer/user/pilot evidence, limitations and retain/modify/remove/consult decisions; revise wording/directions/scoring where warranted, update blueprint and version, and recheck/retest substantive changes where applicable before seeking the responsible authority's final lock/collection decision. Never silently mix versions or treat reliability as proof of validity.

These are process-understanding criteria and a concise plan in the supporting method records, not four mandatory extra questions, extra blank worksheets or a new completed-fieldwork gate. At handoff/delivery state the next real action briefly: keep the draft and submit it to Sir Francis for review and the authorized validation/testing arrangements; do not tell students to recruit or pilot on their own.
