### Concepts to apply

A **research design** specifies the kind of evidence and comparison needed to answer the questions. A **sample** is the group or records actually studied, not automatically the whole population. An **instrument** turns variables into observable answers or records. **Validity** concerns whether evidence supports the intended meaning of an interpretation; **reliability** concerns consistency or precision where applicable. A written procedure can be reproducible without being approved for real-world collection.

### Staged work

1. **Recheck alignment.** Carry forward the approved title, main and specific questions, population/setting, scope, variables, and boundaries from Chapter 1. Make a short question-to-variable-to-evidence map. Where a variable lacks an operational definition or a credible basis, resolve that before finalizing its measurement.
2. **Justify the design.** Choose descriptive, correlational, comparative, experimental, quasi-experimental, or another justified quantitative design. Explain what is observed or assigned, what is compared, and what the design **cannot** establish. An online form is an instrument, not a design.
3. **Define participants and sampling.** Identify target and accessible populations, eligibility, sampling unit/frame, realistic target size, recruitment/selection steps, nonresponse handling, and generalization limits. Do not label convenience selection as random or rely on a sample-size formula without justification.
4. **Build the instrument and its blueprint.** Decide whether to adopt, adapt, or construct it. For every research question, trace *indicator → item or record field → response meaning/unit → code or score → intended analysis*. Attach the entire instrument, directions, answer options and scoring rules on a separate sheet.
5. **Establish instrument quality.** Record genuine content review, comprehension/pretesting, pilot or suitable reliability evidence **as applicable**, with dates, reviewer roles, findings, limitations and changes. If a check is pending, label it **pending**. A signed form alone does not prove validity; one coefficient alone does not prove an instrument valid.
6. **Plan ethical collection and data handling.** State required school/access authorization, voluntary consent/assent or other legitimate records basis, privacy precautions, administrator, neutral invitation, storage/access, incident response, and disposal. Write the chronological collection protocol and a realistic plan for coding, descriptive statistics and, only if justified, bivariate analysis.
7. **Write Chapter 3 and review readiness.** Use the headings required by the school, covering design, setting/population/sample, instrument and its quality, intervention *if any*, data collection, ethics, and analysis. Another reader should be able to understand the exact procedure without inventing missing steps.

### Major questions

- Which data would provide an answer to **each** specific research question? What will count as a valid response or record?
- Why is the selected design more appropriate than a tempting alternative?
- Who is eligible, how will they be selected, and to whom might the result reasonably apply?
- What do the instrument items actually measure? What evidence supports their clarity, intended interpretation, and consistency?
- What approvals and protections are required **before** a person is approached or a restricted record accessed?
- What analysis would answer each question, and which causal or general claims would be unjustified?
