# Activity 1: From research problem to proposed concept note

Apply `../GENERAL-TUTOR.md` and `AREA-INSTRUCTIONS.md`. This is one activity with two entry modes: `INITIAL` and `REVISION`. Revision is another attempt at this same activity, not a new activity or an instruction to repeat every stage.

## 1. Purpose and scope

Develop a coherent, feasible quantitative research concept note containing context, a research problem, proposed title, questions, variables/measurable focus, study type, target population, and proposed data-gathering procedure. Demonstrate the thinking behind the decisions, not merely polished text.

This is a concept-stage planning checkpoint for teacher review. It does not replace the curriculum's full learning modules, Topic Feasibility and Title Decision Brief, Chapter 1 milestone, or later methodology/readiness assessment. No official grade or fieldwork approval is assigned here. No deadline is assigned. The next mapped activity is `pr2_act_02`, available only after explicit teacher approval for Chapter 1 drafting.

Proposed design, access, instruments, and procedures must be plausible and aligned; actual participant permission, a validated instrument, a final statistical plan, final sample size, full RRL, and collected results are not deliverables for this activity. Clearly label future decisions pending. If a teacher-approved prerequisite is required by a particular learner's route, verify it rather than waive it.

## 4. Stable checkpoints and evidence requirements

Use these IDs in feedback records, revision directives, and the private submission audit. C0 is the added context checkpoint; existing IDs are retained so earlier routing references do not shift. Do not require students to learn the IDs.

| ID | Checkpoint | Evidence needed to mark satisfied |
|---|---|---|
| C0 | Context | Relevant setting/group and situation, the observation or experience motivating the concern, and why it matters; distinguish personal observations, assumptions, and verified source claims; no invented local facts, prevalence, or literature gap |
| C1 | Research problem | A specific concern grounded in the context, what needs to be understood, who benefits and why; observations and assumptions distinguished; no invented prevalence, local statistics, or literature gap |
| C2 | Variables and measurement | Each necessary variable/focus explained, a plausible indicator and expected data form, and a suitable source; roles justified by the question rather than forced independent/dependent labels |
| C3 | Target population and access | Intended population, relevant setting/eligibility, plausible accessible group/source, unit of observation, and preliminary selection/recruitment route; proposed access distinguished from permission |
| C4 | Research questions | One main question and a small coherent set of specific questions (normally 2–4, fewer only if substantively sufficient); each linked to a variable/focus, obtainable evidence, and the intended purpose; no filler questions to meet a count |
| C5 | Study type/design | Quantitative approach and a justified proposed descriptive, comparative, correlational, experimental/quasi-experimental, or other appropriate design; what will be observed/compared/assigned and the claim limit explained |
| C6 | Proposed title | A student-developed title matching the focus, purpose, population, and defensible claim; wording is bounded and measurable rather than impressive or causal without support |
| C7 | Proposed data-gathering procedure | Before–during–after outline identifying who supplies what evidence, proposed tool/source, recruitment/permissions/consent path, collection steps, basic privacy/storage plan, and handling of refusal or incomplete data; no claim that tools or access are already approved without evidence |
| C8 | Integrated concept-note review | All eight requested components agree; consequential revisions propagated; the student explains substantive choices in their own words across the session; essential meaning/translation issues resolved |

Initial suggested order: C0 → C1 → C2 → C3 → C4 → C5 → C6 → C7 → C8. Adapt to evidence already present; it is not a mandatory repeated questionnaire. Title ideas may be noted early and finalized after questions/design stabilize. A one-variable descriptive study is acceptable when justified. Do not demand a relationship, intervention, hypothesis, significance question, or artificial second variable.

Use open questions and the general tutor's brief sandwich feedback. For each checkpoint, record actual evidence, assistance, and the specific unmet criterion. Do not fill substantive blanks yourself. Do not require full essays when accumulated short responses demonstrate understanding.

## 6. Targeted revision: reopen only what changed

For REVISION, build a small working ledger from the baseline and teacher directive:

`checkpoint_id | baseline status/evidence | directive or detected dependency | current status | required repair | resolution`

Use statuses: `Retained`, `Open`, `Recheck`, `Satisfied`, and `Teacher clarification required`. Retained means preserved from the identified baseline, not newly demonstrated in this session. Keep teacher approval distinct from tutor verification.

1. Preserve a baseline copy and its version/revision ID. Apply teacher-requested changes as open issues; do not erase previously satisfied work.
2. Reopen the checkpoints named in the directive. Ask one focused question about the first unresolved criterion, not the initial topic-generation question.
3. Recheck only downstream or bidirectional links actually affected by the change. A recheck is a consistency comparison first; ask the student only if a genuine gap or contradiction appears.
4. If a newly exposed issue falls within authorized scope, develop/repair it and record why it was reopened. If it would change a teacher-locked decision beyond the authorized scope, stop that change and seek teacher clarification.
5. Retain unaffected content verbatim except permitted nonsubstantive edits. Do not ask for repeated defenses of already evidenced decisions or rerun all checkpoint interviews.
6. Complete a focused C8 alignment review using retained baseline evidence plus new revision evidence. If the baseline lacks evidence required to verify a mandatory criterion, ask only for that missing evidence; do not invent it or restart everything.

### Dependency checks, not automatic rework

| Changed element | Inspect for possible impact |
|---|---|
| C0 context | Problem relevance, population/setting, feasibility, questions, title, and collection circumstances; recheck only where the context change affects them |
| C1 problem/purpose | Context alignment, variables, population, questions, design, title, and procedure |
| C2 variables/indicators | Questions, design, title, data source/tool/procedure; problem if focus changed |
| C3 population/access | Problem scope, questions/title population wording, design feasibility, selection and procedure |
| C4 questions | Problem alignment, variables/indicators, design, population, title, and evidence collection |
| C5 design | Question/claim wording, variable roles, population/selection, title, and procedure |
| C6 title only | Compare against the established concept; reopen substantive fields only if the proposed title actually changes them |
| C7 procedure | Variable measurement, question answerability, design, access and safeguards; title only if study scope changes |

A grammar-only revision does not trigger a methodological restart. A title change from “relationship” to “effect” is substantive and requires design/claim checking. Do not treat a directive to “revise questions” as permission to replace the topic, population, or approved design.
