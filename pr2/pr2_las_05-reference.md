### Target

Turn **genuine, authorized, checked data** into accurate displays, suitable statistical analysis and findings that answer the research questions. Produce a coherent Chapter 4. A polished graph cannot substitute for actual collection records or a trustworthy dataset.

### Concepts to apply

**Data cleaning** checks records without erasing evidence of problems. The original data remain protected and unchanged; corrections to the working copy must be logged. **Descriptive statistics** report distributions, counts, percentages, typical values and variation. A **bivariate analysis** examines a defensible difference or relationship between two specified variables, when the research question, data type and assumptions permit it. Statistical significance, practical importance and causation are different claims.

Use **N–N–C–E–L** to explain a pattern: **Notice → Name → Cite values → Explain → Limit**. For a findings paragraph, use **R–E–I–L**: **Result → Explanation → Implication → Limit**. These are reasoning supports, not substitutes for the actual statistical method.

### Staged work

1. **Verify collection authority and provenance.** Confirm the exact instrument and protocol used, clearance/status, genuine collection or lawful secondary-data source, dates, and where protected records are kept. If collection was blocked or did not occur, state **not collected**; do not substitute fictional classroom data for the actual study.
2. **Reconcile the participant or record flow.** Report eligible, invited/selected, consenting where applicable, responding, excluded, and analyzed counts with reasons. Explain why the number of valid cases may differ by research question.
3. **Prepare a checked working dataset.** Keep the original data in its approved secure location. Define variables, permissible values, missing codes, scales and derived scores in a codebook. Check duplicates, skipped items, out-of-range values and inconsistent responses. Log material corrections and freeze the version used for analysis.
4. **Match research questions to displays.** Choose tables or graphs based on the variable: frequency tables/bar charts for categories, appropriate distribution displays for numeric variables, and scatterplots for paired numeric relationships. State units, denominators, sample sizes, categories and notes. Do not use a pie chart for overlapping multiple-choice selections.
5. **Analyze without overclaiming.** Report appropriate descriptive values first. Where justified, apply an approved difference or relationship procedure, check assumptions and record the number of cases, statistic, uncertainty, effect and hypothesis decision as applicable. Do not force a significance test on a descriptive-only question.
6. **Interpret each question.** Present the exact finding, identify its important pattern, explain what the numbers mean for this population and measure, and preserve unusual results, missingness and limitations. Compare relevant findings with **verified literature** from Chapter 2; agreement is not required.
7. **Check reproducibility.** Ensure every number in the text, table or graph can be traced to the frozen dataset and calculation. A different reader should be able to find the dataset version and reproduce the result with an appropriate authorized route.

### Major questions

- Are these **actual authorized data**, and can their provenance and collection conditions be verified?
- How many usable cases support each reported result? What was excluded or missing, and why?
- Which table, graph or statistic best answers each specific research question?
- What is the most important observable pattern, including an exception or uncertainty?
- Does the finding show only a description, a difference, a relationship, or a justified causal effect?
- How does the finding relate to the literature without forcing it to agree?
