# Practical Research 2 | Six-Stage Recovery LAS Map and Teacher Gates

**Status:** Proposed classroom recovery implementation aligned with existing PR2 modules and 20-meeting direct-instruction recovery. The six-stage sequence is a reorganization of the *same study*, not a new curriculum or extra set of graded outcomes.

## Six stages, with the first two already completed

| Stage | Focus | LAS handling | Key research evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | Research problem | **Already completed**; retain the approved proposal/problem | Feasible problem, title direction and initial evidence need |
| 02 | Chapter 1 | **Already completed**; retain approved or revision-marked version | Background, problem statement, questions, scope and delimitations |
| **03** | **Chapter 3 and Instrument** | **New LAS 03** | Design, sample, question–instrument–analysis chain, quality evidence, ethics, methodology and clearance status |
| **04** | **Chapter 2: frameworks and RRL** | **New LAS 04** | Verified theory decision, conceptual diagram/explanation, definitions, hypothesis decision, MAPS/TEAL synthesis and citations |
| **05** | **Chapter 4: findings and analysis** | **New LAS 05** | Real collected data, participant flow, checked dataset, tables/graphs, suitable analysis, bounded discussion |
| **06** | **Chapter 5: conclusions and recommendations** | **New LAS 06** | Evidence-linked conclusions, proportionate actions, integrity/coherence audit and completed research evidence portfolio |

**Chapter headings:** Follow the school-approved chapter organization for where the theoretical and conceptual frameworks appear; stage order controls the research workflow, not an imposed manuscript template.

**Why Chapter 3 is early:** It makes the data requirements and collection procedures concrete while there is still time to obtain approvals and collect data. Chapter 2 literature writing then continues in parallel with *authorized* collection. This does not mean research questions or measures may be invented before reviewing any scholarly basis.

## Dependencies that may not be skipped

1. **Chapter 1 question lock** before serious instrument design. A significant question change requires checking all downstream sections.
2. **Minimum literature and conceptual basis** before final instrument lock: identify the variables, meaningful indicators, and source-based justification. Full RRL prose may wait until Stage 04.
3. **Genuine quality evidence and permissions** before each applicable validation, pilot, access, recruitment or fieldwork activity. The school/adviser and other responsible authorities determine what is needed, including guardian consent and assent when minors are involved. A signed LAS is not permission.
4. **No post-start silent instrument change.** Changes discovered while writing Chapter 2 must be reviewed, versioned, and evaluated for whether new authorization, data collection or analytic restrictions are necessary.
5. **Data integrity before Chapter 4:** preserve raw evidence, track participant flow, codebook, cleaning decisions and the final analyzed dataset.
6. **Actual findings before Chapter 5:** simulated data or an incomplete collection route can support practice, but not a claim that an actual quantitative study was completed.

## Competency coverage (teacher only)

- **Already completed stages:** Original foundational competencies Q1-LC01–Q1-LC09 should be verified from existing evidence; do not automatically assume mastery from a submitted cover page.
- **Stage 03:** Q2-LC01–Q2-LC07, with Q1-LC10–Q1-LC12 as essential *provisional conceptual/measurement links*.
- **Stage 04:** Q1-LC10–Q1-LC13: theoretical/conceptual framework, definitions, conditional hypothesis, literature review and synthesis. Recheck Chapter 3 alignment.
- **Stage 05:** Q2-LC08–Q2-LC10: actual data collection, truthful presentation and appropriate descriptive or justified bivariate analysis.
- **Stage 06:** Q2-LC11–Q2-LC12: conclusions and recommendations. Coherence audit revisits all earlier competencies.

## Teacher verification at each checkpoint

| Stage | Teacher should hear the group explain | Blocker requiring revision or escalation |
|---|---|---|
| 03 | Why this design/sample/instrument/analysis is the right chain, what the measure means and which claims are prohibited | Missing quality evidence or approval; instrument/protocol mismatch; privacy or consent gap |
| 04 | Difference between theoretical and conceptual framework; how two relevant sources support, differ or limit a theme; how the variable is measured | Unsupported theory, source that was not actually checked, framework/Chapter 3 contradiction |
| 05 | How one reported number travels from actual record to dataset to analysis to table and conclusion; what is uncertain | Unverifiable real data, inconsistent denominators, inappropriate test or overclaim |
| 06 | Which exact finding justifies each conclusion and action; which limitation matters most | Invented or unsupported result, conclusions unrelated to questions, recommendations without evidence |

## Assessment boundaries

Continue to use the existing PR2 written-work and performance-task destinations rather than treating every table as a new score. The original assessment sequence includes source/synthesis work (WW03, PT02), methodology decisions and readiness (WW04, WW05, PT03), actual results (PT04), and final manuscript/conclusions (PT05). The recovery proposal substitutes a **Completed Research Evidence Portfolio** for a fully formatted manuscript *only if the teacher/school formally accepts the revised final-output rubric*. Earlier approved work is incorporated for coherence and not scored twice.
